Magnetic navigation · tust_helical_li2024

TUST helical robot

TUST micro magnetically guided helical robot for guidewires (MMGHR): magnet in a helical casing with a soft structure ahead of a Nitinol guidewire, driven by a rotating field in a 3D Helmholtz coil
Tianjin Key Lab of Integrated Design and On-line Monitoring for Light Industry and Food Machinery and Equipment, College of Mechanical Engineering, Tianjin University of Science and Technology (P. Li, Z. Wang, C. Ye, D. Fang, J. Zhang); C. Liang, Dept. of Electronic Engineering, CUHK, and School of Mechanical Engineering, Tianjin University (li2024_helical p. 1) · 2024
Family
Magnetic navigation (magnetic-navigation)
Devices
own guidewire head: radially magnetised cylindrical NdFeB magnet (2 mm diameter) in a titanium helical casing (pitch 1 mm, helix angle 20 deg), a silicone soft structure joining it to a Nitinol guidewire (about 100 mm long in the tests); the selected Robot-6 has d1 = 2 mm, d2 = 2 mm, d3 = 0.1 mm (Sec. 2.2.3, 4.1.1, Table 2, Fig. 10)
Degrees of freedom
steering by the direction of the rotating field; helical propulsion by field rotation; no external feeding or twisting (Sec. 2.2, 5); DOF count N.R.

Testing

Highest level
Phantom
What was tested
bench: platform check with a T-shaped permanent magnet, max error < 3 deg (Sec. 3.2, Fig. 9); eight robot configurations in 6 mm tubes at 0 to 15 deg, liquid-free and glycerol-filled (Sec. 4.1.1, Figs. 10-12); steering at guidewire insertion lengths of 1, 2 and 3 cm with and without the soft structure (Sec. 4.2, Figs. 13-14); in vitro phantom: Y-, U- and Omega-shaped glycerin-filled tubes, selective, continuous and large-angle steering (Sec. 4.3, Figs. 15-19); repeat counts N.R.
Key numbers
soft structure raises the guidewire steering angle by 22 / 16 / 32 deg at 1 / 2 / 3 cm insertion, Robot-6 reaches 90 deg against 58 deg for Robot-5 (Sec. 4.2, Abstract); Robot-6 top speed 4.24 mm/s liquid-free and 2.9 mm/s in glycerol (Sec. 4.1.1); step-out frequency 10 Hz (2 mm heads) and 9 Hz (3 mm heads) in glycerol; 3 mT uniform field at 6 A (Sec. 2.1)

Mechanism

Push (translation)
magnetic (helical propulsion: the casing turns with the external rotating field and advances the guidewire head, Sec. 2.2.3); no external feeding mechanism, 'relying exclusively on the propulsive force generated by the robot at the distal end' (Sec. 5)
Twist (rotation)
magnetic torque of the rotating field on the radially magnetised magnet turns the helical casing (Sec. 2.2.2, 2.2.3)
Device range
head 2 to 3 mm diameter, guidewire d3 0.1 or 0.2 mm, guidewire length 100 mm in vessel models; real guidewires are up to 1000 mm and out of scope (Table 2, Sec. 4.3, 5)
Target anatomy
'vascular interventional surgery': guidewire navigation of curved blood vessel bifurcations (Abstract); stent and drug delivery named as potential uses (Sec. 6); tested in glycerin-filled Y-, Omega- and U-shaped tubes (6 mm inner diameter, 50 mm models) standing for the iliac artery, aortic arch and distal coronary arteries (Sec. 4.3, Fig. 15)

Sensing and interface

Force sensing
none
Leader and haptics
none: field magnitude, direction and rotation frequency set in real time by the operator through LabVIEW with camera feedback (Sec. 3.1)
Imaging
optical: HIKVISION MV-CA016-10UM industrial camera above the coils (Sec. 3.1); fluoroscopy N.R.
Sterility
N.R.; the discussion advises a thin biocompatible silicone coat over the magnet, silicone and titanium tip (Sec. 5)

Size

Footprint (mm)
3D Helmholtz coil, coil radii 90 / 145 / 200 mm (x / y / z), turns 50 / 80 / 111 (Table 1); uniform working space 60 mm cube at 6 A (Sec. 2.1)
Mass (kg)
N.R.

Notes
REV4 2026-11-23: included after the full assessment of the 19 candidates (REV4_19_candidates.md §7). The robot is the guidewire's head: the paper does not feed the wire from outside and says so as the main limitation (Sec. 5). The field of 3 mT is much lower than the 15-20 mT of the other magnetic rows (coils built for a 60 mm workspace).
