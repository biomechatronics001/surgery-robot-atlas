CardioXplorer

CardioXplorer open-source modular teleoperated robotic catheter-ablation system
King's College London, School of Biomedical Engineering & Imaging Sciences (K. S. Rhode, R. J. Housden; Z. Xu, A. M. Zeidan) with Guy's and St Thomas' and St George's hospitals · 2024
Family
Academic bedside robots (academic)
Devices
commercial ablation catheters through a catheter-agnostic handle gripper: Thermocool SmartTouch SF and CS, Webster CS EZ Steer (Biosense Webster), Blazer II XP (Boston Scientific) (Sec. 2.1)
Degrees of freedom
3 (insertion/retraction, handle rotation, knob)

Sim-to-real
None found — No simulation-trained controller found for this robot.

Testing

Highest level
Phantom
What was tested
benchtop targeting of left pulmonary veins in an AF-patient cardiac phantom; 3 cardiologists and 2 trainees, 5 trials, manual vs robot (Sec. 3)
Key numbers
task duration -1.59 s with the robot; robotic motion 35.2% smoother for experts than intermediates; jerk improvement vs manual 42.2% (experts) and 13.6% (intermediates); speeds up to +-25 mm/s, 0.5 deg rotation steps up to 9.375 rps; parts cost about USD 700 (abstract, Sec. 2)

Mechanism

Push (translation)
linear-stage+clamp (linear module: NEMA-17 stepper with GT2 belt drive carries the handle, 220 mm insertion depth)
Twist (rotation)
NEMA-8 stepper (19:1) rotates the handle without limit through a slip ring; servo (12 kg.cm holding torque) turns the knob +-60 deg
Device range
four commercial ablation catheter models (open-source CAD handles)
Target anatomy
atrial-fibrillation catheter ablation (pulmonary vein isolation)

Sensing and interface

Force sensing
none on the robot (knob torque of about 6 kg.cm measured during design)
Leader and haptics
joystick, no haptics
Imaging
NDI Aurora EM tracking in tests; phantom imaged on a Siemens Artis Q biplane
Sterility
detachable disposable single-use catheter; N.R. further
