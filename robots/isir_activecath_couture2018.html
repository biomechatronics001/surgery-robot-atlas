ISIR SMA active catheter

ISIR SMA active catheter: three parallel metallic beams with wire-shaped SMA actuators, two bending sections, inside a standard 6F catheter
Institut des Systemes Intelligents et de Robotique (ISIR), Universite Pierre et Marie Curie / Sorbonne Universite, Paris (J. Szewczyk); Service de Chirurgie vasculaire, Hopital Pitie-Salpetriere (T. Couture); in vivo test at the Ecole de Chirurgie (UPMC) (couture2017_design / arXiv 1906.01309, title page, In-vivo section) · 2018
Family
Actuated-tip devices (actuated-device)
Devices
own active catheter: core of three parallel metallic beams (Terumo Radifocus 0.018 in guidewires) with 0.2 mm SmartFlex SMA wires (Memry), two bending sections (L 24.5 mm and 19 mm, 5 mm rigid tip), covered by a commercial Envoy 6F catheter (Codman); the guidewire is handled by the operator's hands (Results, Dimensioning; Control architecture)
Degrees of freedom
2 active bending sections (proximal and distal, one SMA actuator each); insertion and rotation by hand 'like a conventional catheter' (Methods, Design; Control architecture)

Testing

Highest level
Animal
What was tested
bench: 70 prototypes compared to size the core and SMA wires; 13 of them used to check the bending-angle model (Fig. 19); 280 cycles at 2 A in a 37 C water bath (Fig. 18); phantom: Elastrat silicone aorta with water-glycerol 60/40 flow, right femoral approach: all supra-aortic trunks and both renal arteries (90, 120, 150 deg) cannulated, contralateral common iliac failed (Experimental validation, Fig. 13); animal: one 23-kg pig under general anaesthesia and fluoroscopy, five copies of the catheter, all attempted cannulations successful (both renal arteries, celiac trunk, supra-aortic trunks), no perforation, dissection, embolus or thrombosis on angiography; contralateral iliac not reached (In-vivo experiments, Fig. 21)
Key numbers
best prototype: distal bend 110 deg (spec > 90), proximal 70 deg (> 65), minimum radius of curvature 16.4 mm (< 15 spec, not met), reachability Ls 74.1 mm (> 40) (Table 6); model relative error < 20 % for 100-150 deg over 13 prototypes (Fig. 19); stable to 280 cycles, no significant change of external temperature (Fig. 18); activation window 0.8 A / 4 V to 2 A / 10 V (Control architecture)

Mechanism

Push (translation)
tendon/tip-actuation: SMA wire actuators heated by 0.8 to 2 A (open-loop linear feedforward on current, PWM period 20 ms) bend the two sections; insertion by hand (Control architecture)
Twist (rotation)
by hand: the catheter 'can be manually rotated like a conventional catheter' (Methods, Design)
Device range
active device 1000 mm long, OD 0.98 mm inside the 6F (Table 6); specification: > 650 mm and < 2 mm (Table 4)
Target anatomy
'endovascular navigation'; cannulation of the peripheral arteries: supra-aortic trunks, celiac trunk, superior mesenteric artery, renal arteries, common iliac arteries (Methods, Anatomical considerations); silicone aorta model and a 23-kg pig reached supra-aortic trunks, celiac trunk and both renal arteries

Sensing and interface

Force sensing
none
Leader and haptics
no haptic leader: foot pedal board as the human-machine interface so that the hands stay on catheter and guidewire (Control architecture, Fig. 16)
Imaging
X-ray fluoroscopy guidance and angiographic control after each cannulation in the pig (In-vivo experiments); operator adjusts the bend by X-ray feedback
Sterility
N.R.; active parts are sealed inside a standard 6F catheter 'for safety purposes' and a glucose flush between them limits heating (Abstract; Discussion)
