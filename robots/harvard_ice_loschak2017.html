ICE catheter robot

Harvard 4-DOF robotic ultrasound (ICE) catheter steering system
Harvard SEAS (R. D. Howe; P. M. Loschak, A. Degirmenci) with MIT Lincoln Laboratory (L. J. Brattain) and Beth Israel Deaconess Medical Center (C. M. Tschabrunn, E. Anter) · 2017–2020

Testing

Highest level
Animal
What was tested
2017: benchtop water tank with gelatin left-atrium phantom; 2020: one 64 kg live pig, US catheter via a 14 Fr femoral-vein introducer into the right atrium, target 6 Fr quadpolar catheter in the left ventricle, heart paced at 120 bpm; volume-imaging steps (18 steps, 133 s), instrument tracking (329 s) and tracking a manually steered target (431 s) (IJRR 2020 Sec. 3.2-3.3)
Key numbers
benchtop position RMS 1.9 mm; imager spinning 0.25 deg RMS with 1.0 mm tip drift; 3-D pointing 1.6 mm and 0.17 deg RMS; test speeds <= 1 mm/s and <= 0.5 deg/s; 6.5 W brushed DC motors with Maxon EPOS2 (TRO 2017); in vivo 1.05 mm and 1.33 deg mean absolute error with respiratory compensation (IJRR 2020 abstract); in vivo step imaging 2.56 mm / 0.98 deg MAE over 134 s of well-modelled breathing, 6.59 mm / 2.27 deg when the model was outdated; manual-target tracking 2.81 deg MAE and 2.14 mm position MAE over 431 s (Sec. 3.3)

Mechanism

Push (translation)
linear-stage+clamp (lead-screw translation stage carries the whole handle)
Twist (rotation)
motor grounded to the stage turns the handle by timing belt; two handle-mounted motors turn the knobs by timing belts
